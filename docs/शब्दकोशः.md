# शब्दकोशः — संस्कृता's technical glossary

*पारिभाषिकशब्दकोशः · संस्कृतम् – हिन्दी – English*

Every technical term संस्कृता uses, with its Sanskrit derivation, its Hindi
equivalent, and its English counterpart. This exists for three reasons:

1. **So the vocabulary is arguable.** A term you can trace to a root is a term a
   scholar can correct. Each entry below shows where the word comes from, not
   just what it means.
2. **So translators and teachers have one list**, instead of inventing terms per
   classroom.
3. **Because the list is itself a contribution.** Sanskrit has had technical
   registers for grammar, mathematics and medicine for two thousand years; a
   computing register is a reasonable next one. This is a first draft of it,
   offered for revision — corrections are welcome as pull requests.

**Register:** deliberately simple — early-Gītā / Pañcatantra level, not
scholastic. A student with school Sanskrit should be able to read code aloud.

**Method (design rule #12):** keywords are verb imperatives (वद = "speak!",
मानय = "consider!") or nominatives (फलम् = "the fruit/result"). The standard
library follows the धातु principle: names derive from ~50 core roots, so
learning one root opens a family of words.

**Status:** drafted by the project; a formal paṇḍit review is budgeted before
v1.0 and has *not* yet happened. Nothing here should be cited as reviewed usage.

## 3. Kārakas — the six argument roles (कारकाणि)

Pāṇini's kāraka theory assigns each participant in an action a role. संस्कृता
uses the same six as **named parameters**, and the order of arguments at a call
site does not matter.

| कारकम् | Pāṇini's sense | हिन्दी | English role | Programming analogue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| कर्ता | the doer | कर्ता | agent | who performs |
| कर्म | that which is most desired by the doer | कर्म | object | what is acted on |
| करण | the most effective means | करण | instrument | with what |
| सम्प्रदान | the recipient | सम्प्रदान | recipient | to whom |
| अपादान | the fixed point of departure | अपादान | source | from where |
| अधिकरण | the locus | अधिकरण | location | in / at what |

Note (design risk #9): kāraka labels are **explicit syntax** (`कर्म:`), never
inferred from word endings. The engine does zero morphology. Pāṇini inspires the
design; he does not parse at runtime.

## 7. Terms we deliberately did **not** Sanskritise

Loanwords are honest when the concept itself is imported and universally
spelled: **जेसन** (JSON), **python:** (the bridge prefix), and library names
reached through the bridge (`गणितम्.sqrt`). Inventing a Sanskrit word for a
proprietary format name would be decoration, not clarity.

## Contributing a correction

Found a term that is grammatically wrong, ambiguous, or needlessly obscure? That
is exactly the feedback this file is for. Open an issue or a pull request with:

- the current term,
- your proposed term,
- the derivation (root + affix, or the classical attestation),
- one sentence on why the current one is wrong.

Terms already used by the engine can only change in a major version, but the
glossary can record the better term immediately, marked as pending.

**References followed:** CSTT (Commission for Scientific and Technical
Terminology) practice for Hindi technical vocabulary, and the classical grammar
convention of deriving from a root with an explicit affix rather than
transliterating.
