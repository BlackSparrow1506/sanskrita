# Code of Conduct — आचारसंहिता

## सङ्गच्छध्वं संवदध्वम्

> *सङ्गच्छध्वं संवदध्वं सं वो मनांसि जानताम्।*
> "Move together, speak together, let your minds be of one accord."
> — ऋग्वेदः १०.१९१.२

That verse is the whole policy. The rest of this document is only what it means
in practice for a repository.

## The standard

**Expected of everyone here:**

- Assume good faith. A confusing pull request is usually a busy person, not a
  bad one.
- Argue with the code, the design, or the grammar — never with the person.
- Say what you mean plainly. Directness is not rudeness, and politeness is not
  agreement.
- Accept correction the way we ask others to: when someone shows you are wrong
  about a Sanskrit term, an algorithm, or a claim in the README, thank them and
  fix it.
- Explain your reasoning. "This is wrong" helps nobody; "this is wrong because
  `अनुक्रमः` returns ० when absent, so this comparison is off by one" does.
- Remember who else is reading. Many contributors here are students, and many
  are working in their second or third language. Write so they can follow.

**Not acceptable, in issues, pull requests, discussions, or anywhere else this
project happens:**

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks
- Discrimination or demeaning comments based on caste, religion, region,
  nationality, language, gender, sexuality, disability, age, or appearance
- Mocking someone's Sanskrit, Hindi, or English — including mocking their
  *lack* of Sanskrit. Not knowing the language is a reason we exist, not a
  disqualification
- Sexual attention or imagery
- Publishing someone's private information
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions without
  new evidence, or demanding unpaid work from maintainers

## On religion and politics

Sanskrit carries three thousand years of religious and political weight, and
people arrive here with strong and opposing feelings about that. **This
repository is about a programming language.** Discussion of Sanskrit's history,
grammar, and register is welcome and necessary. Using the project as a platform
for religious or political advocacy — of any kind, in any direction — is not.

Two concrete rules that follow from this:

1. **We do not disparage other languages, human or programming.** English gave
   programming to the world; Python and Java taught most of us to code. Our goal
   is for संस्कृता to stand on its own merits, not for anything else to fall.
2. **Technical claims need technical evidence.** "Sanskrit is the best language
   for computers" is not a design argument. "Kāraka roles let arguments be
   order-independent without positional ambiguity, and here is the parser that
   does it" is.

## Scope

This applies in the GitHub repository, in the project's videos and their comment
sections, and anywhere someone is representing संस्कृता.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to **langotegouri@gmail.com**. Reports are read by the
maintainer and kept confidential. You will get a reply within 7 days.

Responses, in escalating order: a private word, a public correction, editing or
removing the content, a temporary ban, a permanent ban. Which one is used depends
on severity and on whether it has happened before. The maintainer will not act
on a report about a conflict she is a party to without asking an uninvolved
contributor to look at it first.

If you think a decision was wrong, say so by email. You will get a reasoned
answer, though not necessarily the answer you wanted.
